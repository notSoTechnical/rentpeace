🗓️ How a typical case moves

  1. 0Day 0 — Notice goes out, with proof

    We prepare the Notice of Intent to File (DC-CV-115) and send it by Certified Mail. The proof of mailing / mailing certificate comes back to you, and Maryland's 10-day wait runs from a mailing you can prove. Get this paper wrong and the filing after it stalls — so it goes out right.

  2. 1Day 10 — Complaint filed

    DC-CV-082 filed with the District Court, backed by your notice and its proof of mailing; you get the case number the same day.

  3. 1~Days 15–30 — Hearing

    We appear for you. Uncontested cases usually get judgment for possession + rent owed that day. The tenant generally has 4 days to appeal or pay to redeem.

  4. 2After judgment — Warrant

    If the tenant hasn't paid in full or left, we file the Warrant of Restitution (Step 2) and the sheriff schedules the eviction.

  5. 3Eviction day

    Eviction notification mailed & posted (Step 3), crew on site with the sheriff, locks changed — property back in your hands.

  6. 5After move-out — Collections placement

    If a balance remains, we close out the final accounting — rent through move-out, utilities, documented damage — and place it with I.C. System under your own account (Step 5).

Court dates, appeal windows, weather holds, and sheriff backlogs are outside anyone's control. What we control — filing the moment you're eligible — we do the same day.

Questions

Before you ask

Why does the notice matter so much? Can't I just mail it myself?

You can — plenty of landlords do. The risk is in the details: the Notice of Intent to File (DC-CV-115) has to name the right tenants, at the right address, for an amount your ledger actually supports, and the Failure to Pay filing can't be made until the required notice period has run. A defective notice doesn't get fixed at the hearing — it stalls or kills the filing, and the clock starts over while the rent loss grows.

Our Step 0 service prepares the notice from your lease and ledger, sends it by Certified Mail, calculates your earliest filing date from the mailing, and delivers the proof of mailing / mailing certificate back to you — so when the case is filed, the paper underneath it holds.

Can I buy just the notice service?

Yes. Step 0 is $40 on its own — DC-CV-115 (or the correct notice to vacate for your case type) prepared, Certified-Mailed, with proof of mailing returned to you — and it's included at no extra charge inside every Step 1 filing package. If you already sent a notice yourself, tell us in the intake: we'll look at what you sent before anyone relies on it, and re-issue it if it won't hold.

Are you lawyers? Can an agent really appear in court for me?

No — we are not attorneys, we are not a law firm, and we do not give legal advice. Maryland Real Property § 8-401 expressly allows a Failure to Pay Rent complaint to be brought by “the landlord or the landlord's duly qualified agent or attorney,” and the District Court's own form DC-CV-082 provides for a “Landlord's Agent” to appear. With your signed written authorization, we file and appear for uncontested rent cases.

How long does the whole thing take?

Most uncontested cases take about 22–45 days from notice to judgment, and longer to a physical eviction depending on the sheriff's schedule in your county. The 10-day notice wait and the court's hearing calendar are fixed by law and the court — anyone who guarantees you a specific eviction date is guessing. What we guarantee is same-day action whenever a step becomes available.

What if the tenant pays after I start?

Good — that's the cheapest outcome there is. In most Failure to Pay cases the tenant holds a right of redemption: paying the full judgment amount (plus any costs the court allows) before the eviction stops the process. Tell us the moment any payment arrives, partial or full, so the court record and the balance stay accurate — a partial payment is credited, it doesn't wipe the balance unless you agree in writing that it does.

What if my tenant fights the case?

If the tenant contests — claims serious repair failures, disputes the lease or the amount, or demands a jury trial (which moves the case to Circuit Court) — that is attorney territory. We stop, tell you exactly what was raised, and refer you to a licensed Maryland landlord-tenant attorney for the contested portion (typically $250–$1,000). Roughly 95% of rent cases never get there.

Which counties do you cover?

Regular coverage: Prince George's, Montgomery, Anne Arundel, Howard, Charles, Frederick, Calvert, St. Mary's, Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Harford, and Carroll. Another Maryland county? Ask us in the intake form below — if we can't cover the court date responsibly, we'll say so before you pay anything.

Do I need a rental license or lead certificate?

Some jurisdictions — Baltimore City in particular — expect a valid rental license (and, for older properties, lead registration/certification) and a judge can postpone or dismiss a case without them. Send us what you have with your intake; we check your documents before filing so a missing paper doesn't burn your hearing date.

Can you collect the money judgment too?

A possession judgment and a money judgment are different things — a money judgment generally requires the tenant to have been personally served or to appear. We'll tell you which kind your hearing produced.

For the balance itself, that's our Step 5 — Collections Placement: we build the final accounting — rent through the move-out date, utilities billed to the tenant, and documented damage, minus payments and any deposit applied — and place the account with I.C. System under your own I.C. System account. I.C. System works it on contingency (its fee comes out of recoveries under your agreement), recovered funds remit directly to you, and you keep 24/7 access to your account data. Our part is the first-party work — the accounting, documentation, and placement — for a $75 flat placement fee; we are not a collection agency and never place your tenants' debts under our own account.
